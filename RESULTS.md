Only the action encoder and decoder depend on the action dimension; the 4-arm head is a synthetic widening (action_dim 32 → 64, `speed/arms_2v4/widen_action_head_onnx.py`) — timing only, no accuracy claim.

**fp8 isolated on the same board** (Thor fp8 vs Thor bf16): 18/20 same outcome, flips [8, 10], arm rel_mae 0.101. Thor fp8's flips vs the 5090 decompose into one hardware/stack flip (seed 7, which also flips under Thor bf16) and two fp8-precision flips (seeds 8 and 10).

**fp8 really ran:** 391 Linear layers converted, 664,200 `torch._scaled_mm` calls with torch.float8_e4m3fn operands over the 900 policy calls. 664,200 e4m3 GEMMs / 900 policy calls = 738 per call = 165 (SigLIP) + 84 (Qwen LLM, select_layer 12) + 1 (mlp1) + 24 (VL self-attn) + 4 x 116 (DiT per Euler step). Attention, norms, patch-embed and the per-embodiment state/action encoder+decoder stay bf16.

closed-loop latencies are PyTorch eager policy calls (not TensorRT): board compute, and in-loop incl. host<->board ZMQ. On Thor, fp8 is slower than bf16 at batch 1 (dynamic-quantisation overhead): in-loop incl. ZMQ ~141 vs ~76 ms; board compute in the table. Use results/speed for deployable latency.

Caveats:

- The 5090 reference is bf16, not fp32 ground truth.
- n = 20 seeds, one noise draw per seed: Wilson 95% CIs are about +/-20 points and overlap for every backend (all ~40-50%). The data rule out a collapse, not a 10-20 point degradation; the fp8 > bf16 rate ordering is noise.
- The checkpoint authors report 0.60 for this task (SOURCED: Isaac-GR00T examples/RoboCasa/README.md at the pinned commit); our 45% (Wilson CI 26-66%) is consistent with it.
- Seed 14's initial observation is nondeterministic on the host simulator (3 distinct call-0 hashes over 7 host sweeps; not the seeded layout RNG). It is a win on every backend and in no flip; excluding it leaves the conclusions unchanged (Orin 8/19 = 5090 8/19; Thor fp8 9/19 vs 8/19).
- The 5090 reference run predates call-0 hashing, so its scene identity is inferred (host probe + call-0 action agreement <=0.013 vs ~0.30 for a different scene). The 2026-09-18 board runs hash-match each other on 19/20 seeds (all but 14); the 2026-10-12 Thor bf16 re-run also differs at seed 5, which is not a flip seed.
- Thor fp8 determinism was not tested (no repeat run); Orin fp16 is bit-exact across two processes.
- Thor fp8's flips are heterogeneous: seed 8 is a true boundary case; seed 10 is a large-divergence success (per-call rel_mae peaks at 1.70) that won the seed the 5090 lost.

Reproduce: `accuracy/run_closed_loop.py` per backend, then `accuracy/finalize.py` and `accuracy/make_summary.py` (docs/ACCURACY.md). The raw runs (per-seed success, every action chunk, server call logs) are in `results/accuracy/runs/`; `finalize.py` recomputes every number in this table from them.

## 7. Arms vs cameras: what actually scales (RTX 5090, MEASURED)

Only the action encoder + decoder grow with the number of arms. Re-run 2026-10-12 (`speed/multiarm/camera_token_scaling.py`, TensorRT 11.2.1.2): 0.0539 ms per module call, 0.2156 ms per policy call at 4 denoise steps = 3.52% of the action head. This differs from §4 (TensorRT 10.16, engines built separately) and from §2's in-pipeline encoder/decoder times (measured inside a full policy call, including hand-offs): three methods, same conclusion -- arms are a rounding error next to the backbone.

| 2 | 2.559 | 2.3662 | 512 | 2.908 | 2.8246 |

## 8. GEAR-SONIC whole-body controller: the 50 Hz loop on each computer (MEASURED, 2026-10-13 / 2026-10-14)

**Falls at the band drop.** The script releases the robot from the simulator's elastic band 2 s into each run. Some runs fell right after: unloaded, Thor 3/10, Orin 1/10, RTX 5090 0/3 (first reset 0.75–1.22 s after the command in each of these runs). The affected runs show no timing signature (every step under 2 ms around the drop). Against the 5090's 0/3 neither count is significant (Fisher exact two-sided p = 0.528 Thor, 1.0 Orin; DERIVED). The controlled band-drop test (§8.1) answers it: with the release time controlled, the RTX 5090 falls too, at the same rate as the Jetsons, with on-time control.

### 8.1 Controlled band-drop test (MEASURED, 2026-10-14)

Falls are counted by the D1 rule. Every release landed within about one 5 ms physics step of its target (actual delay minus D: RTX 5090 0.39–5.02 ms, Jetson AGX Thor 0.43–4.97 ms, Jetson AGX Orin 64 GB 0.32–4.80 ms); no run was excluded.

So the band-drop falls are a property of the drop transient in this sim2sim harness, not of one computer's speed or timing (INFERRED from the MEASURED counts).

**Caveats.**

- Closed-loop runs can repeat bit for bit: the controller and simulator are deterministic given identical event timing. Comparing simulator-state fingerprints (per-sample sha256 of the joint state, stored in each run file), RTX 5090 bd_D025_r1 / bd_D025_r3 share the 2 s before the drop, then diverge; Jetson AGX Thor bd_D025_r1 / bd_D080_r2 share the 2 s before the drop, then diverge; Jetson AGX Orin 64 GB bd_D025_r1 / bd_D025_r3 are identical for 80 s from the release (both fell); Jetson AGX Orin 64 GB bd_D040_r1 / bd_D080_r1 share the 2 s before the drop, then diverge; Jetson AGX Orin 64 GB bd_D040_r2 / bd_D100_r3 share the 2 s before the drop, then diverge. The runs are therefore not independent trials, and the Clopper-Pearson intervals and Fisher tests, which assume they are, are optimistic.
- Falls are counted by the D1 reset rule. The simulator resets only when the pelvis drops below 0.2 m, so a robot that falls at the drop and lies with its pelvis just above that is reset seconds later and D1 files it as *later* (Thor bd_D040_r3, Orin bd_D010_r1). A body-state check (tilt above 60° or pelvis below 0.30 m after the command; added after the first such run) agrees with D1 on every run (18/18, 18/18, 18/18), and puts every fall run down 0.68–1.16 s after the command.
- 3 runs per D: a strong D dependence would have shown, a weak one cannot be excluded. A controlled delay does not control the robot's sway phase at release.
- Stock deploy only; the simulators keep their first-campaign command intake (RTX 5090: upstream DDS listener; Jetsons: polled readers).

**Exploratory, not pre-registered:** on Thor the three fall runs left the band moving faster sideways (horizontal pelvis speed at release 0.263, 0.141, 0.190 m/s vs a median of 0.070 m/s in the other 15; exact permutation p = 0.027, DERIVED). The same test does not separate the falls on Orin (p = 0.158) or on the RTX 5090 (p = 0.724). A hypothesis for a future pre-registered test, not a result.

### 8.2 Late-start fix on the Jetsons (MEASURED, 2026-10-14)

## 9. GR00T as a drone's flight brain (MEASURED, 2026-10-14 / 2026-10-15)

Same brain, new body: GR00T N1.5's frozen vision-language backbone (camera image + instruction in) with a small action head trained to fly a simulated quadrotor to a named pillar (16 velocity setpoints per decision, 20 ms apart). No diffusion transformer: the head replaces GR00T's action head. One decision = raw camera frame + prompt + drone state in, setpoints out, host work included (*full cost*). Method, scripts and how to run it: [drone/README.md](drone/README.md). Every number below is rebuilt from `results/drone/raw/` by `drone/make_drone_table.py`; setup, software and caveats: `results/drone/drone_setup_2026-10-15.json`.

**Target.** 30 decisions per second (Hz) is our design choice, not a standard: on the full-cost rates, any target from 23 to 40 Hz gives the same verdict (alone: the RTX 5090 and Thor meet it, Orin does not; during the inspection burst only the RTX 5090 does). We picked 30 Hz for this drone: it flies at about 2 m/s and takes a setpoint every 20 ms, so 30 decisions per second keeps each plan less than about 7 cm of flight old. In the simulator the reach numbers hardly depend on speed (it waits for the policy, or applies the delay exactly); on a real drone a slow brain flies on stale plans.

**Caveats.** Simulation only: a kinematic drone (velocity setpoints integrated every 20 ms, no aerodynamics), a toy scene with four coloured pillars, ~2 m/s, a 0.8 m goal. The flight controller that would track the setpoints is not modelled. The policy is a frozen GR00T backbone plus a small behaviour-cloned head, not a GR00T fine-tune; a 3B vision-language-action model is far heavier than the navigation networks production drones fly. The 50 MP inspection AI on the flight computer is a stress case: real drones usually run it on a payload computer or after the flight. The stand-ins have random weights: their outputs mean nothing; only their compute shape and the gated nav decisions matter. Route planning duty is ASSUMED. Reference = the RTX 5090's eager bf16 policy (what the head was trained on), not ground truth; bf16 is itself 0.023σ from fp32. Closed loop: the simulator never runs while the policy computes; the delay arms apply the latency in simulator time instead. With 40 paired episodes only large reach differences are detectable. RTX 5090 clocks were not locked, and its host CPU is hybrid: the full-cost decision takes about 7.2 ms with the Python thread on a P-core and about 8.6 ms on an E-core, and the OS moves it every few seconds. Short unpinned runs can land in either state; pinning (the pinned rows) removes it. NVIDIA's full TensorRT path synchronises after every engine and uses no CUDA graphs; a fused export could be faster (not measured). Predictions for every arm were written down before it ran; they are not reproduced here.
